# Phase 6 mathematical audit

The claims concern fixed customer order, a prescribed depot and vehicle load, a feasible deterministic battery trace, an initially full battery, deterministic travel and service, and a linear station-specific charge time. The benchmark uses independent synthetic lognormal waits, a zero clock at departure, stationary laws at each visit, and disabled station operating hours. A station visit consumes a wait even when its recorded charge is zero. All time units are native to the data. The following mathematics is conditional on this model.

## One stochastic visit

At step (i), let (t) be the previous departure, (d_i) travel, (r_i) ready time, (u_i) service or charging duration, and (w_i) the station wait or zero. The frozen order is

```text
arrival = t + d_i
start   = max(arrival, r_i) + w_i
end     = start + u_i
```

Apply customer deadlines to start and the depot deadline to end. Write their accepted cutoffs as (D_i^s,D_i^e), with infinity when absent. In real arithmetic each finite cutoff includes (10^{-9}). The actual NumPy start comparison subtracts the deadline, whereas end compares to the rounded sum. The analytical helper uses the greatest representable start cutoff allowed by the subtraction near the boundary. The composed max-plus envelope models the real-arithmetic recurrence with those cutoffs. Different floating operation orders can still shift a computed waiting threshold by a few ulps. Exact equality tests use direct replay at numerically critical boundaries. This difference matters for deterministic point masses and for interpreting equality to a rounded displayed threshold.

Before the station set ((a,b)=(-\infty,0)); the clock is (\max(a+w,b)). Travel adds (d_i) to both terms. A ready floor (r_i) sets (b\leftarrow\max(b,r_i)). At the station, the incoming clock is deterministic. Assign that clock to (a) and set (b=-\infty); adding (w) creates the affine term. Duration adds (u_i) to both terms. A deadline (\max(a+w,b)\le D) requires (b\le D) and, when (a) exists, (w\le D-a). Intersect every constraint with (w\ge0). A failed constant condition or a negative cap makes the interval empty. Otherwise the feasible set is ([0,\tau^*]), including a zero cap or infinity. The cap concerns a stochastic visit and applies even if its energy is zero.

Independently, invert post-station steps backwards. Start with latest admissible terminal departure (L=+\infty). Each step requires (\max(t+d_i,r_i)\le K=\min(D_i^s,D_i^e-u_i,L-u_i)). If (r_i>K), no incoming clock works. Otherwise update (L=K-d_i). At the station include its start/end bounds, subtract duration and the deterministic start-before-wait, and compare the resulting cap to zero. Test the preceding prefix separately. This is equivalent to the forward envelope over real arithmetic because each inversion preserves exactly the set of admissible incoming departure times.

The frozen `isolated_caps` omits the readiness feasibility tests, uses zero-wait trace station departure, clips negative values to zero and adds `1e-10 * steps * max(1, abs(latest), abs(departure))`. Under zero-wait feasibility at every downstream step, ready-floor constants already meet their downstream constraints. Then no delay in the feasible set can violate a constant branch. For one visit, dropping downstream floors in the reverse inequality leaves the same ideal cap (prior to floating-point operations and the cushion). The same reasoning holds for an isolated visit with other waits zero, even if the full plan has several visits. The cushion generally makes the implemented cap greater; infinite caps coincide and the arithmetic can round away a small cushion. Downstream ready times affect the zero-wait trajectory and the shape of completion even in examples where they do not alter the deadline cap.

For (W\sim\mathrm{Lognormal}(\mu,\sigma^2)) with (\sigma>0), positive finite cap gives (\Phi((\log\tau^*-\mu)/\sigma)). Empty or zero cap gives probability zero, infinite cap gives one. For (\sigma=0), use the deterministic wait with equality accepted. The code handles overflow without forming unbounded exponentials for the nondegenerate CDF. Zero visits gives deterministic feasibility and return, separate from the evaluation of source-exposed paths. Multiple visits do not reduce to this one-dimensional formula.

If final return is (T(w)=\max(a+w,b)), a missing floor or (k=b-a\le0) gives (E[T]=a+\exp(\mu+\sigma^2/2)). For positive (k), (E[T]=b+E[(W-k)_+]). Integrating the normal density after completing the square gives

```text
E[(W-k)+] = exp(mu + sigma²/2) Phi((mu + sigma² - log k)/sigma)
           - k Phi((mu - log k)/sigma).
```

The implementation evaluates the difference in log space and returns the limiting floor if terms are indistinguishable at float64 precision. Overflow returns infinity when the expectation exceeds the representable range. The deterministic case evaluates the clock at its single wait. The formula averages the full return including failed trajectories. Independent normal quadrature is a numerical test, and the empirical means and uncertainty from actual archived-route replays are a separate check.

## Minimum energy at a single visit

Let (e_0) and (e_1) be the energy to and from the visit. With full initial battery (Q), arrival energy is (B=Q-e_0). Over real arithmetic physical feasibility is (e_0,e_1\le Q) with (0\le q\le Q-B) and (q\ge e_1-B). Thus (q_{\min}=\max(0,e_1-B)). Cumulative consumption is nonnegative and increasing, so segment endpoint checks suffice in this model. The frozen code permits up to NUM_TOL at each residual and at capacity, treats an explicit small negative amount as zero, interprets `None` as full recharge, and charges zero duration when energy added is at most NUM_TOL. The tolerant continuous inequality is (q\ge e_1-B-\text{NUM\_TOL}). Boundary replay uses nonnegative explicit amounts with a 1e-11 energy cushion when needed, and reports the candidate convention. Changing q does not change the wait distribution. Charge time is nondecreasing in q for positive rate, and the downstream clock is monotone in station departure. A larger amount cannot improve any deadline or decrease unconditional completion. A readiness floor can make the comparison a tie. This argument does not select quantities jointly at several stations, where shifting charge between rates can change both completion and feasibility.

AS1 uses a zero-wait prefix for every customer gap and backwards max-plus suffixes. An inserted visit replaces the direct gap leg with two legs, and scanning all admissible station IDs leaves the remainder unchanged. Link extents determine the minimal energy; uncharged candidates retain the same station wait. Every selected route is validated. This is exhaustive enumeration of the specified restricted model with the documented numerical boundary convention. It does not solve the multi-visit EVRPTW.

## Marginal bound, finite-tape screening and QBR

Every success under a nonnegative vector of waits stays successful after reducing every visit but (j) to zero, by coordinatewise monotonicity of every deadline clock. Consequently its (j)-th wait must satisfy the isolated necessary cap. The event of success is contained in each cap event, so (R\le\min_j F_j(\tau_j)). No independence is required for this minimum. Under independence the intersection of all necessary events also has probability (\prod_j F_j(\tau_j)). The frozen screen uses the minimum, not this product. For a deterministic wait check inclusion at the capped boundary. An empty station list supplies neutral bound one on an already valid trace. The floating cushion makes the bound conservative for the archived scales; comparisons at the edge remain contingent on finite-precision clock arithmetic.

If the true upper bound lies below 0.95, the true plan chance constraint cannot be met. A finite sample can still score above target. For example, construct a one-visit plan with isolated cap one and choose `mu = -norm.ppf(.94)`, `sigma=1`. Then (P(W\le1)=0.94). The explicit 512-row sequence with each wait 0.5 achieves empirical reliability one. The event that all 512 draws fall below one has probability (0.94^{512}>0). The frozen optimizer tests its screen only once its valid empirical incumbent has `score[1]==1`; it rejects when the upper bound is below `target-1e-12`. Neither this screen nor QBR certifies finite-sample attainment. QBR tests a vector of visit quantiles at (0.95^{1/m}) for (m) visits. If that vector satisfies all timing constraints, independence gives probability at least 0.95 for coordinatewise smaller waits. This is a sufficient implication for the modeled fixed plan with exact quantiles. The implemented heuristic need not find such a plan.

## Prefix rule

For any valid plan, the zero-wait completion (C_0) lower-bounds the full completion of each nonnegative-wait trajectory. For a prefix of (k=128) from the fixed (S=512)-row tape, (m) successes and completion sum (C_k) imply (R_S\le(m+S-k)/S) and (\bar C_S\ge(C_k+(S-k)C_0)/S). The score is ((1,1,-\bar C_S,R_S)) at or above target and ((1,0,R_S,-\bar C_S)) below it. If the upper reliability misses target, every continuation misses and both tuple components are bounded in order. If the upper rate attains and the actual rate misses, the class component alone settles the comparison. If both attain, the optimistic negative completion is higher or tied, and optimistic reliability bounds its tie-break. The frozen implementation further subtracts (10^{-8}) from the completion lower bound. This protects common rounding differences at the observed scale and is not a floating error bound for arbitrarily large clocks. With the same tape and incumbent, rejecting an optimistic tuple less than or equal to the incumbent cannot discard a strict improvement, including ties. Changes to budget consumption or wall time can change which future proposals are reached.

The ordinal tape generates independent seeded columns. Lengthening it preserves old rows, and extending one cache entry from 128 to 512 charges 384 incremental draws. A new trial, including CON, is charged before evaluation. There are at most (64\cdot512=32{,}768) incremental draws in c64 and archive counts were checked against that limit. The c64 131,072 ceiling therefore cannot bind under that accounting. The separately frozen 8,192-scenario case can bind. Draw savings alone do not establish equal quality or wall-time improvement.

## Fixed topology MILP

For each consecutive station pair or terminal interval, the wait-free recurrence composes to (\max(A+t,B)). The segment also has an input deadline (L), with a separate check that constants generated by floors satisfy deadlines. At each station, add incoming travel, its sampled wait and (q/r). Introduce an epigraph departure variable per station and final return for every scenario, plus a zero-wait scenario with all timing constraints compulsory. Any epigraph solution can only delay the actual earliest replay. Minimum objective in the return epigraph gives the exact earliest unconditional mean for a fixed charging vector. Read the exact simulator replay, not the possibly slack intermediate solver clocks.

Before station (j), accumulated prior charge minus consumed energy plus (Q) bounds the leg's terminal battery below by (-\mathrm{NUM\_TOL}). The corresponding postcharge is bounded above by (Q+\mathrm{NUM\_TOL}). At the depot impose the lower bound again. For each scenario, recursively upper-bound station and return times using zero start, the full known waits, each segment's max-plus form and (Q+2\mathrm{NUM\_TOL}) per stop. An optional input deadline uses (M=\max(0,U-L)), where (U) is the corresponding bounded preceding epigraph clock with a scale-dependent cushion. Thus a binary failure relaxes only the deadline and does not skip completion. No independence is needed to formulate the fixed sample model. The linear time approximation on explicit amounts at or below NUM_TOL differs from the frozen deadband by at most NUM_TOL divided by rate per visit. This and float tolerance limit certificates to the specified linear SAA model; only exact physical and timing replay supports comparisons. Solver statuses and independent validation must be read separately.

## Independent evidence

The synthetic tests cover ready-floor absorption, an empty interval, a zero cap, an infinite cap, a float boundary disagreement in a naïve cutoff implementation, degenerate and extreme waits, stop-loss quadrature, minimal and larger feasible quantities, exhaustive gap enumeration, success-implies-cap for multiple visits, threshold-crossing prefix arrays, and valid finite big-M under an extreme scenario. Both archived and new validation tapes of the selected experimental plans are rechecked on five scalar scenarios with frozen energy and timing semantics. Read the final audit for actual counts, failures, and any model-level restrictions. These results do not establish novelty in the literature, validity for other stochastic environments, global optimality, or a fleet-level chance constraint.
